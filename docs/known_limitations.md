# Known limitations

0.x: breaking renames are allowed (no deprecation cycle). See [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). Compiler bugs that change results are in [known_issues.md](known_issues.md).

| Topic | Status |
| ----- | ------ |
| API stability | 0.x — breaking renames allowed |
| Example coverage | Teaching demos for the main paths; not one example per REFERENCE row |
| H∞ synthesis / mixsyn, μ | Not implemented; `norm_hinf` and SISO / loop-at-a-time disk margins ship |
| Motor / power / motion packs | Not in this public tree; the FOC slice (`foc.hpp`, `modulation.hpp`, `spm.hpp`) is the exception |
| No C++ standard headers | The core builds on ETL with `ETL_NO_STL`, math `FREESTANDING` or `HEADER` ([README](../README.md#using-etl-instead-of-the-standard-library)). `make nostd-check` emulates this with `-nostdinc++` on GCC; also checked by hand on Clang 19 and 23 and arm-none-eabi. Not tried on a toolchain that truly ships none, such as avr-gcc. `damp/workbench.hpp` still needs the standard library |
| Delay in a design | On the input, in the design: the sampled designs (`state_feedback`, `kalman`, `steady_state_target`, `mpc`) take the continuous plant and the `InputDelay`, `design::state_feedback(sys, Ts, weights, noise, InputDelay<N>{θ})`, N·NU delay states after the plant's, one delay per channel or one for all. On the measurement, in the filter's design: `kalman`, and `state_feedback` and `mpc` with a `KalmanNoise`, take the `OutputDelay`, M·NY delay states the filter's alone, the model `discretize(sys, Ts, InputDelay<N>{θ}, OutputDelay<M>{θ})` with NX + N·NU + M·NY states. An LQGI's integrators integrate the late reading: they agree with the gain's model at a steady state, not in a transient. Every disturbance reaches a late reading exactly, as it was when the reading was taken: an estimated d as a state of the plant held over the period, through its B_d and C_d (an `InputDisturbance`'s through u), and a `MeasuredDisturbance`'s as the plant moved under it. An `InputDisturbance` on a delayed plant reaches it through the delay states, and its walk moves x only through them: exact for a delay of a sample or more, it leaves out the part of the last period after a shorter delay's switch. A `constexpr` design's `Law` pays only for the delay states' nonzero terms; a runtime holding its matrices pays a dense square of the state per tick for a filter. Every channel gets the longest delay's delay states. On a discrete model with the delay built in, a fraction of a sample on the measurement is exact for the noise held over each period (discretize's convention for G), not for white noise of a density; the design option's filter takes the density exactly. Many samples of dead time: sample slower, or `SmithPredictor`. A delay that changes from tick to tick is not modelled. An LQ design, and an MPC its Ru and RΔu, books the input cost on the command: exact for each input's own weight, not for an R that couples inputs of different delays, and a cross term N on a delayed input is refused (`bad_weights`) |
| Limits and anti-windup | One contract ([guide](guide.md#limits-and-anti-windup)), with these gaps: `SwitchedController` starts a law without a tracking mode (anything but a PID) from its reset state; of the disturbance estimates only `OFLQG`'s and `OFMPC`'s have a limit (`disturbance`, through `set_limits()`), not `ESO`'s, `ADRCController`'s or `ClassicalDOB`'s; `STSMCController`'s integral state holds against `u_lim` but has no limit of its own; a `DemandLimit` is a heuristic on the gain with no stability certificate while it binds. An integrator limit is the I term in output units on a PID (`integral_lim`) and ξ ≈ ∫e·dt on the LQ laws (`IntegralAction::limits`). An `InputRateLimits` is per sample, on an MPC and on an LQ law, and `SlewLimiter`'s rates per second |
| NaN and ±Inf | A parameter (a gain, cutoff, time constant, sample time, limit or config field) that is NaN or ±Inf is refused, as each documents, under `-ffast-math` too; +Inf is refused wherever the value must be finite, and unbounded is `numeric_limits::max()`. A `StateSpace` whose `Ts` is NaN, ±Inf or negative is neither discrete nor continuous, and the designs refuse it (`invalid_sample_time`). Signal values on a tick are not checked: a NaN reading gives a NaN command, so the firmware should check its readings (`damp::isfinite`, which holds under `-ffast-math`) before it ticks a law |

Implementation notes:

- GCC: designs of about 10 states or more at compile time need `-fconstexpr-ops-limit=67108864` (2^26); smaller designs fit the default. Clang's default `-fconstexpr-steps` runs out sooner, at a few states (a 2-state plant with `InputDelay<5>` is 7): raise it, `-fconstexpr-steps=67108864`.
- GCC 11: `Mat2 A = {{…}}` does not deduce its scalar type; write `Mat2<double>`. GCC 11.2 and older cannot assign a `std::optional` at compile time, which some designs do.
- Clang 18 to 23, inside a template (a function template, a member of a class template, a generic lambda): a type deduced from its fields without a deduction guide deduces nothing from a designated field set to a literal or to a constant declared outside the template ([llvm/llvm-project#114870](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/issues/114870)). `design::IntegralAction{.Q = 80.0}` there is `IntegralAction<>`, and the design does not compile; `LQRWeights{.Q = kQ, .R = kR}`, `MPCWeights`, `DisturbanceModel` and `Bounds` fail alike, and so do the limit options with an end set to such a constant (`InputLimits{.upper = kUpper}`, a `ColVec<2>` declared outside, is `InputLimits<1>`; the same for `InputRateLimits`, `OutputLimits`, `StateLimits`), and a `DemandLimit` whose ends are `float` literals stays `DemandLimit<M, double>`. A field computed in the template (`diag(1.0, 2.0)`, `Matrix<2, 2>{…}`, `ColVec<2>{…}`, `2.0 * q`) or an index list in braces deduces, and so do the types with deduction guides (`KalmanNoise`, `StateSpace`, `InputDelay`, `OutputDelay`). A braced end deduces nothing on either compiler, anywhere: `InputLimits{.upper = {1.0}}` is one input in `double`. In a template, name the type: `IntegralAction<double>{.Q = 80.0}`, `LQRWeights<2, 1>{.Q = kQ, .R = kR}`, `InputLimits<2>{.upper = kUpper}`, `DemandLimit<2, float>{…}`. GCC deduces all of them.
- Placed designs (`state_feedback` with `Poles` or `ObserverPoles`): placement assigns a pole at most once per input (per reading, for a filter), as `design::place` does, and a deadbeat delay line is a defective block. So a gain with an `InputDelay` of two or more delay states per input on a plant with several inputs is refused (`not_placeable`), and so is a filter with an `OutputDelay` of two or more per reading and several readings; one input (one reading) takes any delay, and a filter's `InputDelay` any, since its delay states are not corrected.
- Clang 23 with `-ffast-math` on x86: float `asin` and `acos` reach 1.4e-6 near |x| = 1. Clang 19 and non-fast-math builds hold the documented 3.1e-7 and 4.8e-7.
- Recursive filters, covariance in float: `KalmanFilter` and `ExtendedKalmanFilter` carry the triangular factor S of their covariance (P = S Sᵀ, `estimation/sqrt_covariance.hpp`), which holds a prior 10¹² times a reading's variance in float. The others still store P itself, the form that lost it in `KalmanFilter` once cond(P) passed about 1/eps (in float, a prior about 10⁵ times a reading's variance on a state plus a sensor bias: P indefinite within a few ticks, nearly every later update refused). Those are `ErrorStateKalmanFilter` (Joseph form) and the INS filters built on it (`sensor_fusion.hpp`, `ins_eskf.hpp`), and `UnscentedKalmanFilter` (P re-factored for the sigma points each tick). The ESKF stays in that form by choice: what fails is a reading of a state plus its own sensor's bias (C = [1 1]), and the ESKF's IMU biases enter through the propagation while its aiding readings carry none. The shipped INS and MARG filters tracked the double filter in float from priors up to 100 km. A square-root draft cost 1.8 to 5.7 times the cycles on a Cortex-M4F, since F mixes the bias rows into the dynamic ones and the triangularization of [F S, Q½] is dense where F P Fᵀ runs on F's pattern. MHE has no running covariance, but its arrival weight is the inverse of P∞, formed from P.
- Recursive filters, readings: on the factor a perfect measurement (R = 0, or a zero-variance row of R) leaves eps times its prior variance in what it reads, not zero, and one repeated with no process noise in between is refused once that is down to the rounding of the factor. `KalmanFilter::step` returns the state; a refusal of its update is in `last_status()`. No filter exposes its innovation or its NIS. A NaN in a reading reaches the estimate, not the covariance; a NaN in C or R is refused.
- Arduino: Damp's headers go before or after `Arduino.h`. After it, code outside Damp still reads `PIDMode::PI` as Arduino's `PI` macro (name the controller `PIController`), and a sketch's `abs()` is the standard library's, since Damp undefines Arduino's macro.
- ESKF: thin-H Joseph path. Kalman/ESKF: some large-Δt Φ assumptions (documented in headers).
- Host analysis: some FRF helpers continuous-only unless routed via discrete `StateSpace`.
- Model inverse (`design::output_inverse`): square only, with a vector relative degree. A zero within √ε of the unit circle is refused (the −1 sampling zero of a ZOH-sampled double integrator).
- MPC: a `StateLimits` with an `InvariantSet` does not compile, since the set's rows do not cover the states. On an estimated state (OFMPC) the bound holds on the estimate's prediction: a load the filter has not caught yet carries the plant past it. `set_limits` moves only the outputs and states the design's `OutputLimits` and `StateLimits` list: to move one at run time, list it (its `lim` may start unbounded). A runtime spelled out by hand with either names their counts and needs its solver sized for the extra rows; `MPC law{res}` and `Law<kRes, T>` size it.
- MPC: the QP weighs and predicts every output of the plant as y = C·x, so an output it weighs or bounds must have D = 0 (`bad_weights`); a reading with feedthrough is measured but left out of the cost, a zero row and column of Qy and Qy_terminal and no `OutputLimits` row. An MPC with a `KalmanNoise` and no disturbance spec designs, its target holding a `TrackedOutputs`, but has no runtime: `OFMPC` runs with a disturbance to estimate. `to_ss()` is the law without its target: with Ru, a terminal ingredient or held outputs its r and d gains leave out the pull toward the target.
- Measured disturbance (`MeasuredDisturbance`): d is held over each period, as the input is, and on an MPC over its horizon too: a d known ahead has no preview, and the law is the steady state under the d it reads now. A law whose r is not its target's holds every output at 0 under d (a regulator) or its integrated outputs (an LQI), so a regulator whose outputs no steady state can hold under d is refused (`singular`), and one with more outputs than inputs does not compile: list the ones to hold with `TrackedOutputs`, r = 0.
- Steady-state target (`steady_state_target`, and every law that holds r through one): at most one held output per input. A design whose target would hold more, a `TrackedOutputs` or a spec's `.outputs` longer than the inputs, or one that lists none on a plant with more outputs than inputs, does not compile; MATLAB® takes the weighted least-squares compromise, which holds none of them. A regulator (no `TrackedOutputs`, no spec: an LQR, an MPC) holds nothing at r and needs no list, until a `MeasuredDisturbance` has it hold every output at 0. `steady_state_target` with neither a list nor a `MeasuredDisturbance` holds nothing and does not compile. An MPC holding every output takes r in the plant's order: a full list in another order is refused (`bad_weights`). `OFMPC` always takes r (its spec's outputs) and has no regulator form, as `OFLQG`.
- MPC: the feedforward `x_ref`, `u_ref` is one point, not a preview: the plan carries it on the model over the horizon, so a planned trajectory that leaves the model's path (a stop the plan has not reached yet) is seen as it comes, tick by tick. The output and state limits stay absolute; one that should ride the reference is moved with `set_limits()` each tick, which recomputes the QP's bound vector.
- MPC: with a hard output row or a terminal set, a MaxIterations or Degenerate iterate that fails the primal row check is held (last applied input). That hold does not restore output or terminal-set feasibility. Soft-output ticks still apply the exhausted iterate, then the u box: `last_status()` is the only sign of it.
- MPC in float: the active set has a margin of about 100 in the slack weight `ecr_weight` over its default of 1e5; past it some ticks end Degenerate. A hard output limit the plan rides at full input can read Infeasible on a tick in a hundred, the rounded state lying past what the input can still reach. `as<float>()` refuses a design whose Hessian does not factor in float (a condition number of about 1e6 or more: a long horizon on an integrating or unstable plant, or a small move weight).

## Stack

Matrices are passed and returned by value, so a routine's peak stack is a multiple of the matrices it works on. A `constexpr` design costs the target nothing; a design run at start-up on the target needs the stack below, and so does a recursive filter's tick. Worst-case call chain in float on Cortex-M4F (arm-none-eabi-g++ 14.2, `-O2`), one input and one output, C library frames not counted:

| Call | 2 states | 4 states | 8 states | 12 states |
| ---- | -------- | -------- | -------- | --------- |
| `discretize(sys, Ts)` | 1.7 KB | 4.2 KB | 12.2 KB | 24.9 KB |
| `design::state_feedback(sys, Ts, config)` | 5.6 KB | 15.1 KB | 47.2 KB | 97.7 KB |
| `design::kalman(sys, Ts, noise)` | 2.8 KB | 9.6 KB | 37.7 KB | 83.7 KB |
| `mat::expm(A)` | 0.6 KB | 2.4 KB | 8.1 KB | 17.8 KB |
| `mat::solve(A, b)` | 0.3 KB | 0.8 KB | 2.1 KB | 4.5 KB |
| `KalmanFilter::update(y)` | 0.2 KB | 0.8 KB | 1.8 KB | 3.7 KB |
| `KalmanFilter::predict(u)` | under 0.1 KB | 0.3 KB | 0.8 KB | 1.8 KB |

The sampled designs take most of theirs in the matrix exponential of the Van Loan block, 2·(NX + NU) rows for `lqr` and 2·NX for `kalman`. `KalmanFilter::update` holds about six NX×NX temporaries. A fixed-gain law or observer (`LQR`, `LQG`, `FixedGainObserver`) holds vectors only.
